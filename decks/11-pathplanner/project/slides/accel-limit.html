Where “Max Linear Acceleration: 11.2” comes from

1

Grip: 160 N per side

Wheel friction × weight on that side: 1.2 × 27.216 kg × 9.8 ÷ 2.

2

Motors: 152 N per side

Two CIMs at 60 A give 18.5 N·m at the wheel. 6.9 N·m goes to friction, judged from true max speed. Divide by the wheel radius.

3

The smaller one wins: 152 N

Two sides push 304 N on 27.216 kg: 11.2 m/s².

4

Slip above 62 A

The app’s Max Optimal Current Limit: past it, the wheels slip instead of pushing harder.

These numbers are computed in this deck’s source from the same formula the app uses, with WPILib’s CIM motor model, and they match the screenshot. Force per side is the smaller of two limits: what the motors can push, and what the carpet can hold before the wheels slip. At true max speed, the motors are only fighting friction, so the current they draw there tells the app how much torque friction eats. That is why true max speed matters. 11.2 m/s² is the robot’s ceiling. The 1.5 m/s² path limit sits far below it, so the path sets the pace, not the motors.
