State-space and LQR are the next step up

State-space control describes a mechanism with a small math model, and kV and kA are already one. LQR then picks the feedback gains from how much error and how many volts you will accept.

The picture: sport and eco modes in a car. You choose the trade-off, not the pedal position.

You have met it: SysId uses LQR for the P and D gains it suggests.

WPILib’s advice: pick the easiest strategy that works on the field.

(1 min) LQR stands for Linear-Quadratic Regulator, and it uses matrices, so explore it with a mentor. With PID you turn dials until it feels right. With LQR you say how far off you can tolerate and how many volts you want to spend, and the math picks the gains. If you use SysId, you are already using it. WPILib’s own advice is to pick the easiest strategy that works, so finish feedforward and PID first. The state-space link on the last slide starts from PID and builds up.
