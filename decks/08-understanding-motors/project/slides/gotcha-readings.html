Common mistake

Comparing a motor limit with a PDH reading

What you see

The motor is stalled at a 60 A limit, but its PDH channel shows about 27 A. One of them looks wrong.

Why it happens

getOutputCurrent() is motor current. The PDH measures battery current. At low speed they are far apart.

The fix

Compare getOutputCurrent() with your limit, and the PDH with your breaker. Log both.

SmartDashboard.putNumber("Drive/MotorAmps", leftLeader.getOutputCurrent());

SmartDashboard.putNumber("Drive/AppliedOutput", leftLeader.getAppliedOutput());

SmartDashboard.putNumber("Drive/PdhChannelAmps", pdh.getCurrent(LEFT_LEADER_PDH_CHANNEL));

SmartDashboard.putNumber("Drive/BatteryVolts", RobotController.getBatteryVoltage());

Both numbers are right. This is the two-currents chart, seen on a real dashboard. Those 60 and 27 amps come from our model for a stalled CIM; yours will differ a little. The applied output line is the duty cycle, so you can check it yourself: multiply it by motor current and you get close to the PDH number. Put these four lines in periodic in your drive subsystem, and watch them in Elastic or AdvantageScope. The PDH channel number depends on your wiring, so look at which slot your SPARK uses.
