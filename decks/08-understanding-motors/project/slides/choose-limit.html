Deck 8 · Understanding motors

13

Here is one way to pick a number: ask what the motor needs to do. A drivetrain can only push until the wheels slip, so that force sets the job. Divide it through the gearing to get torque at one motor, then let DCMotor turn torque into amps. We get 60.8 amps. Any more makes heat and wheel spin, not push. WPILib notes many teams gear to slip at 40 to 50 amps per motor. REV’s FAQ suggests starting drive motors at 20 amps and raising it until the wheels break traction. For an arm, start from holding torque at the motor instead, as in Deck 6, and add some margin.
