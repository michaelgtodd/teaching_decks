Deck 8 · Understanding motors

9

Computed from the CIM numbers on an ideal 12 volt supply. Power in is volts times amps. Heat in the windings is current squared times resistance, 0.090 ohms for a CIM. Power out is the hump from before. Near free speed, the little that goes in mostly beats friction. At stall no work comes out, so all 1596 watts become heat in a small motor. Current squared is the key: heat grows much faster than current, so a modest cut in current saves a lot of heat. REV warns stall heat can damage a motor in a fraction of a second if it is not managed.
