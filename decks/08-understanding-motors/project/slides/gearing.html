Gearing

Gearing trades speed for torque: the KitBot

1

Start at the motor

5310 RPM free speed and 2.42 Nm stall torque.

2

Divide speed by the ratio

5310 ÷ 8.45 = 628 wheel RPM.

3

Multiply torque by the ratio

2.42 × 8.45 = 20.4 Nm per CIM at the wheels.

4

One wheel turn rolls one circumference

6 in × π = 18.85 in, which is 0.479 m.

5

Turns per second × distance per turn

628 ÷ 60 × 0.479 = 5.0 m/s (16.5 ft/s).

6

Compare with the real robot

FIRST quotes ~12.7 ft/s (3.87 m/s), 77% of this free-speed math.

Deck 8 · Understanding motors

8

(2 min) Do this on a whiteboard with them. A gearbox is like the low gear on a bike: your legs spin more, and each push counts for more. 8.45 to 1 means the wheel turns once for every 8.45 motor turns. Speed goes down by 8.45, torque goes up by 8.45. Power stays about the same, apart from losses. Then one wheel turn rolls the robot one circumference. We get 5.0 metres per second. Free speed means no load at all. AndyMark’s 12.7 feet per second estimate assumes a loaded CIM at 4100 RPM instead of 5310. Friction and a sagging battery are part of why. Hand math gives you the ceiling.
