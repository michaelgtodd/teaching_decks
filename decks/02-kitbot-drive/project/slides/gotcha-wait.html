Common mistake

A waitSeconds step in a drive sequence

What you see

During the pause, the Driver Station shows DifferentialDrive... Output not updated often enough.

Why it happens

Commands.waitSeconds runs no drive code, so nothing updates the drive. After 0.1 s, motor safety stops it and reports the error.

The fix

Hold the drive still with a step that drives at zero for the pause, as on the slide before.

// Error during the pause

forward.andThen(Commands.waitSeconds(1.0)).andThen(turn);

DifferentialDrive has motor safety turned on by default, with a 0.1 second limit. If arcadeDrive is not called within that time, WPILib stops the motors and prints the error. A wait command requires no subsystem and calls no drive code, so a pause made from it lets the limit run out. Driving at zero for the pause keeps calling arcadeDrive with zero, so the drive is updated every loop and stays still. The message text is from WPILib’s MotorSafety class.
