PathPlanner and Choreo

One lets you draw the path, one solves it

PathPlanner: you shape the curve

Drag Bézier curves in an app. That is a smooth line you bend with handles.

The timing is worked out on the robot, from your speed limits.

It can plan around obstacles during a match.

The follower comes with the library.

Choreo: a solver finds the fastest path

Place waypoints and limits in an app, and a solver does the rest.

The fastest path your drivetrain can do, saved to a fixed file.

Better robot numbers give better paths: mass, motors, gearing.

You write the follower yourself.

(2 min) This is the big difference today. In PathPlanner you are the artist: you bend the curve until it looks right, and the robot works out the timing from your limits. In Choreo you say where to go and what the robot can do, and a solver computes the fastest path. A solver is a program that searches for the best answer. It runs on your laptop before the match, so the path is fixed. One team on Chief Delphi tried it and reported paths about 6 to 11 percent faster. That is one team’s test, not a rule. PathPlanner can also load Choreo paths, so you can add one to the other.
