Common mistake

The onboard arm sits high in the simulator

What you see

In sim, SparkArmSubsystem stops about 4° high at 45° and 12° high at 80°, so moveTo never finishes.

Why it happens

REVLib’s simulator reads the kCos angle as degrees, so gravity help barely fades as the arm rises. REV’s docs say rotations, and that is what goes on the robot.

The fix

Keep 1 / 60. Use the roboRIO versions to practice in the sim, and check kCos on the real arm.

We measured this by running the code in WPILib simulation with REVLib 2026.0.5: a 45 degree goal settled at 48.8, and an 80 degree goal at 91.9. The tempting fix is to change the ratio until the sim looks right. Setting it to 6 does make the sim land on target, and it would be wrong on the robot. So do not tune kCosRatio in the simulator. ArmSubsystem and ProfiledArmSubsystem do their gravity math in Java, and both land on target in the sim. On the real arm, check kCos before you trust it: clear of people, with P set to 0, the arm should barely drift near flat and near 45 degrees.
