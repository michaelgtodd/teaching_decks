Control theory

Where 1.21 volts comes from

1

Gravity torque when flat: 15.7 N·m

Mass × g × distance to the middle: 4 × 9.8 × 0.4.

2

Torque at the NEO: 0.261 N·m

The gearbox divides it by 60.

3

Current for that torque: 10.6 A

Divide by the NEO’s 0.0248 N·m per amp.

4

Volts to push that current: 1.21 V

Multiply by the NEO’s 0.114 ohms. The motor is not turning, so it makes no voltage of its own.

Four steps, only multiply and divide. Gravity pulls with 15.7 newton metres when the arm is flat. The gearbox divides that by 60. The NEO’s torque constant turns torque into amps, and its resistance turns amps into volts. The resistance and torque constant come straight from the stall numbers in WPILib: 12 volts over 105 amps, and 2.6 newton metres over 105 amps. So kG is 1.21 volts. On a real arm you measure it, but this tells you what size to expect.
