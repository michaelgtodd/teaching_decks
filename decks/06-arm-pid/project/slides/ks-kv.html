The rest of feedforward: kS and kV

ArmFeedforward(kS, kG, kV) adds all three. You already know the middle one.

kS: friction

The volts needed to overcome friction and start moving.

kG: gravity

1.21 V × cos(angle) on our arm.

kV: speed

Back-EMF grows with speed. Our arm takes 1.19 V per rad/s of speed.

kS covers friction: the small voltage needed to start moving. Our simulated arm has no friction, so kS is zero here, and your real arm will need some. kV covers speed. A spinning motor generates a voltage against its supply, called back-EMF, so going faster takes more volts. That one also comes from physics: the gear ratio 60 divided by the NEO’s speed constant of 50.4 rad/s per volt gives 1.19. kV needs a target speed, and a setpoint jump does not have one.
